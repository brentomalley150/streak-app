How it works

Simple for everyone
involved.

One daily loop for kids, the controls for grown-ups — the same on every track.

1

Start a challenge

Pick a track and how long it runs — 12 weeks unless you choose otherwise. Keep it to your own kids, or share a link with friends or a whole class.

Every joining parent sees exactly what's shared, and confirms for their own child.
2

Kids build their streak

One tap per activity. Each tile shows its points, ideas and rank names match the theme they picked — from chess and sports to art, space and fantasy — and doing everything in a day earns a perfect-day bonus. Points climb through ranks, plus a weekly bonus activity.

Forgot to log? Kids can fill in any day from the past week.
3

Parents stay in charge

A PIN-protected parent section. Set your own prizes, confirm chores, and add spring test scores so the reading routine starts where your child actually is.

Cashing in a prize never costs a single point.

Safe by design

Made for kids under 13,
and their parents.

Kids don't have accounts — they're profiles under a parent's. And everything they see was written by a person.

✍️

Human-written, no AI

Every routine, idea and weekly bonus comes from lists people wrote and reviewed. Nothing your kid sees is generated, and their data never goes to an AI company.

🙂

First name and a score

That's all a challenge ever shows: a first name, an avatar and points for that track. Never a last name, photo or location.

🔑

Only you can join your kid

A friend or teacher can invite. Only a child's own parent can say yes — one kid at a time — and leave in one tap.

🏠

Scores stay in the family

Test scores live in the PIN-protected parent section. Never shown to your kid or anyone in a challenge, and you can delete them any time.

Declan's story

Declan's mom, Kate, came up with the idea and built this app over a long weekend for her 4th-grade son, who wasn't reading voluntarily. Four weeks later, Declan was opening the app on his own, logging books, and sitting at Knight rank on the leaderboard.

The bet was simple: his reading wouldn't slide backwards over the summer. His fall MAP retest says it didn't — his reading score went up 14 points. We promised to publish the result either way.
