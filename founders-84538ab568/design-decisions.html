What these two decisions actually change

The design system leaves two calls open: purple instead of v1's gold, and keeping the kid's screen dark. Both sound like taste. Neither is. Below, each option is rendered live — real tokens, real contrast ratios, the same screen built both ways. Look at them rather than the argument.

The call

Keep it dark. The kid surface has one job: get a ten-year-old to open it without being asked.

This also settles a smaller question — the PRD's P1 "add a light theme" applies to the parent screens, which are light already. It was never a request to re-theme the kid's game.

The call

Purple leads; gold is reserved for achievement. The last two rows aren't hypothetical — those two greys are shipping on beattheslide.com right now and fail the AA bar your PRD sets.

Note what row 4 shows: gold is excellent on dark at 12.57:1. It isn't a bad color — it's a bad button. Decision 01 is what lets it keep its job.

If you'd rather go the other way

Both calls are reversible, but not equally cheaply. Roughly what each costs if you decide the opposite:

01
Make the kid screen light
Half a day of token work, plus re-verifying every ratio on the new ground — and you lose the game feel that gets a ten-year-old to open it. Cheap in effort, expensive in product.
02
Keep gold as the brand
Cheap today, while there are four HTML files. Expensive once buttons, links and focus rings exist across a real codebase — and it needs an accessibility answer for the 3.19:1 button either way.
→
Either way, fix the two greys
Independent of both decisions. Two token changes on the hub and landing page bring them to the AA bar the PRD already commits to.
